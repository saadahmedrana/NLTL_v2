GENERAL\n\n1.1     Application of the 2021 Ice Class Regulations\n\nThe Ice Class Regulations of 2021 apply to ships contracted for construction on or after 5 July\n2021.\nThe provisions in section 1.8 (Ice classes) and chapter 2 (Ice Class Draught) in the 2021 Ice\nClass Regulations apply to all ships irrespective of their year of build.\n\n1.2     Application of the 2017 Ice Class Regulations\n\nThe Ice Class Regulations of 2017 apply to ships contracted for construction on or after 1 Janu-\nary 2019 but before 5 July 2021.\n\nAs from 1 December 2017, the Ice Class Regulations of 2017 may, however, also be applied to\nships contracted for construction on or after 1 December 2017 but before 5 July 2021.\n\n\n1.3     Application of the 2010 Ice Class Regulations\n\nThe Ice Class Regulations of 2010 (TRAFI/31298/03.04.01.00/2010) apply to ships contracted\nfor construction on or after 1 January 2012 but before 1 January 2019.\n\n1.4     Application of the 2008 Ice Class Regulations\n\nThe Ice Class Regulations of 2008 (8.12.2008 No. 2530/30/2008), issued by the Finnish Mari-\ntime Administration, apply to ships contracted for construction on or after 1 January 2010 but\nbefore 1 January 2012.\n\n1.5     Application of the 2002 Ice Class Regulations\n\nThe Ice Class Regulations of 2002 (20.9.2002 No. 5/30/2002), issued by the Finnish Maritime\nAdministration, as amended, apply to ships the keels of which have been laid or which have\nbeen at a similar stage of construction on or after 1 September 2003 but which have been con-\ntracted for construction before 1 January 2010.\n\n1.6     Application of the 1985 Ice Class Rules\n\nThe Board of Navigation Rules for Assigning Ships Separate Ice-Due Classes, 1985, (2.9.1985\nNo. 2575/85/307), as amended, apply to ships the keels of which have been laid or which\nhave been at a similar stage of construction on or after 1 November 1986 but before 1 Sep-\ntember 2003. On the owner’s request, the requirements of the 2008 Ice Class Regulations\nmay, however, be applied to the engine output of such ships.\n\nHowever, ships of ice class IA Super or IA the keels of which have been laid or which have\nbeen at a similar stage of construction before 1 September 2003 shall comply with the require-\nments in section 3.2.2 or 3.2.4 of the Ice Class Regulations of 2017 not later than 1 January in\nthe year when twenty years have elapsed since the year the ship was delivered.\n\n1.7     Application of the 1971 Ice Class Rules\n\nThe requirements of Annex I or, depending on the ship’s age, section 10 of the Board of Navi-\ngation Rules for Assigning Ships Separate Ice-Due Classes of 1971 (6.4.1971 No.\n\f                                                                                         Regulation\n                                                                                                  6 (65)\n                                                                TRAFICOM/68863/03.04.01.00/2021\n\n\n\n\n1260/71/307), as amended, apply to ships the keels of which have been laid or which have\nbeen at a similar stage of construction before 1 November 1986. On the owner’s request, the\nrequirements of the 1985 Ice Class Rules or the 2008 Ice Class Regulations may, however, be\napplied to the engine output of such ships.\n\nHowever, ships of ice class IA Super or IA the keels of which have been laid or which have\nbeen at a similar stage of construction before 1 September 2003 shall comply with the require-\nments in section 3.2.2 or 3.2.4 of the Ice Class Regulations of 2017 not later than 1 January in\nthe year when twenty years have elapsed since the year the ship was delivered.\n\n1.8       Ice classes\n\nUnder section 3 of the Act on the Ice Classes of Ships and Icebreaker Assistance (1121/2005),\nships are assigned to ice classes as follows:\n\n      1. ice class IA Super; ships with such a structure, engine output and other properties that\n         they are normally capable of navigating in difficult ice conditions without the assistance\n         of icebreakers;\n      2. ice class IA; ships with such a structure, engine output and other properties that they\n         are capable of navigating in difficult ice conditions, with the assistance of icebreakers\n         when necessary;\n      3. ice class IB; ships with such a structure, engine output and other properties that they\n         are capable of navigating in moderate ice conditions, with the assistance of icebreakers\n         when necessary;\n      4. ice class IC; ships with such a structure, engine output and other properties that they\n         are capable of navigating in light ice conditions, with the assistance of icebreakers when\n         necessary;\n      5. ice class II; ships that have a steel hull and that are structurally fit for navigation in the\n         open sea and that, despite not being strengthened for navigation in ice, are capable of\n         navigating in very light ice conditions using their own propulsion machinery;\n      6. ice class III; A Charpy V impact test shall be carried out for ma-\nterials other than bronze and austenitic steel. An average impact energy value of 20 J based\non three tests must be obtained at minus 10 ºC. For nodular cast iron, average impact energy\nof 10 J at minus 10 ºC is required accordingly.\n\n6.4.2      Materials exposed to sea water temperature\n\nMaterials exposed to sea water temperature shall be made of steel or another ductile material.\nAn average impact energy value of 20 J, based on three tests, must be obtained at minus 10\nºC. This requirement applies to the propeller shaft, blade bolts, CP mechanisms, shaft bolts,\nstrut-pod connecting bolts etc. It does not apply to surface-hardened components, such as\nbearings and gear teeth. The nodular cast iron of a ferrite structure type may be used for rele-\nvant parts other than bolts. The average impact energy for nodular cast iron shall be a mini-\nmum of 10 J at minus 10 ºC.\n\n6.5      Design loads\n\nThe given loads are intended for component strength calculations only and are total loads, in-\ncluding ice-induced loads and hydrodynamic loads, during propeller/ice interaction.
